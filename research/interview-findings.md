# Exploratory customer discovery from the earlier team project

Source: three existing student interviews supplied by the portfolio owner from the earlier
question-board / philosophy team project. The service was explained before the recordings.
This portfolio reuses the research context with explicit attribution rather than presenting
the interviews as a newly conducted commercial study. Raw transcripts and recordings remain private.
Only de-identified, paraphrased themes are published; no names, programs, classes or identifying quotes.

## Findings and product consequences

| Observed theme | Product interpretation | Implemented response |
|---|---|---|
| Concern about judgment when asking | Peer visibility can be a barrier | Public projection excludes authors |
| Overload and limited lesson time | Submission alone is not enough | Persistent queue and teacher answers |
| Clear and timely explanations matter | Teacher adoption is necessary | Teacher queue and answer workflow |
| Misuse and trust concerns | Moderation and identity transparency matter | Hide/reopen and explicit teacher-visible notice |

The three interviews are exploratory convenience data. They support problem/feature hypotheses,
not willingness to pay, population percentages, learning improvement or institutional purchasing.
Recognition errors in one transcript mean ambiguous passages are excluded. No quotations are
reconstructed. A classroom pilot and buyer interviews are separate next validation activities.
