# ClearQuestion business and product plan

## Problem and customer segments

Students sometimes avoid questions because of judgment, overload or interruption concerns.
Students are end users; lecturers facilitate; departments or institutions are potential buyers.
The beachhead is one consenting lecturer/class where a small written-question workflow can be tried.
Existing interview research motivates the problem; it does not establish buyer demand.

## Value proposition and alternatives

A simple peer-private question queue with teacher accountability, shared voting and moderation.
Alternatives include spoken questions, LMS forums, chat groups, paper question boxes and existing
classroom polling tools. Differentiation to test: clear identity boundary and low setup burden.
Avoid claims that the product eliminates anxiety or replaces teaching quality.

## MVP and roadmap

The runnable app supports submit, public queue, voting, teacher answers and hide/reopen.
Core acceptance checks are executed in the SA/SQAT snapshots of the same prototype. MVP feedback
should precede institutional SSO, multi-room operation, configurable retention and live push updates.
Access: python app.py, then http://127.0.0.1:8000. The locally generated teacher key grants moderation.

## Market sizing and competition

TAM is all institutions/classes that could use the category; SAM is the reachable segment under
language, deployment and procurement constraints; SOM is a feasible acquisition scenario within SAM.
The calculator uses 200 reachable accounts and 10% penetration purely as a planning scenario.
No sourced population total or market-share achievement is invented. A real market estimate needs
a cited institution count and observed procurement evidence.

## Finance and funding

finance.py computes contribution, CAC, simplified LTV, payback, break-even and churn sensitivity.
Base scenario: monthly price 30, variable cost 6, fixed cost 600, acquisition spend 300 and ten new
accounts, monthly churn 5%. Currency is a consistent illustrative unit; numbers are assumptions.
Reports retain the calculation. Grants, university incubation and founder bootstrapping are options
to evaluate, not funding already received. Key risks are lecturer adoption, procurement and support load.

## Operations, management and PR

Product owner prioritizes evidence; developer maintains releases; research lead handles consent;
lecturer facilitates the pilot. Outsource commodity hosting only after security/privacy review.
Keep core product decisions and customer learning in the team. A balanced scorecard considers
user value, reliable operations, learning/iteration and sustainable cost. PR message: another way
to ask, with transparent identity boundaries. No fabricated endorsements or institutional partnership.
